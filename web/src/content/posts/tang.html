Abstract

Deep learning frameworks reach peak speed through hand-written kernels: one per operator, per backend, per fusion pattern. Each kernel freezes decisions (what to fuse, where data lives, how threads share work) that depend on the model, the shape and the chip. We describe the tensor IR in tang, a Rust differentiable-computing library, which replaces those kernels with two primitives: views, affine maps from an iteration space to tensor coordinates, and comprehensions, reductions under a monoid whose body is a scalar expression graph. Fusion is one substitution rule. Flash attention is not written; it falls out of a rewrite that recognizes online softmax as a monoid, plus one staging rule. Gradients of comprehensions are comprehensions, so the backward pass, including a flash-style attention backward with no intermediates, comes from the same passes. Number formats and thread mappings are schedule fields that cannot change the result, and a per-kernel autotuner chooses among them on the device. The same graphs lower to WGSL, CUDA (with mma.sync tensor cores), Metal (with simdgroup matrices), OpenCL, GLSL and C. On an RTX 3090 a transformer block’s forward pass derived this way runs 1.37× faster than PyTorch with TF32, and a whole Qwen3-8B 4-bit decode step runs as one IR graph at 55% of memory-bandwidth speed of light. The compiler contains no operator-specific code.

1 Introduction

A modern model is a few dozen distinct operators. A modern framework is thousands of kernels, because every pair of (operator, backend) needs one, and every profitable fusion such as attention with its softmax, a matmul with its bias and activation, or a norm with the projection that follows needs another. FlashAttention 1 is the clearest case: a large speedup that came from rewriting one operator by hand so its score matrix never reaches memory. Its backward pass took another hand-written kernel, and each new chip generation takes another rewrite.

Hand kernels are forks. Each re-derives the same arithmetic and freezes a schedule that was right for one shape on one device. We measured how often that schedule is wrong. Causal attention at on an Apple M4 Max is fastest as the naive graph with threads, because the scores fit in cache and parallelism is the bottleneck. At it wants the tiled flash schedule. Matmul at wants a shared-memory tile; at the same tile loses. Rules of thumb tuned on the M4 picked the wrong matmul schedule on an NVIDIA RTX 3090. The winning schedule depends on the graph, the chip and the size, not on the operator.

This paper argues that a whole model should go through an IR small enough to reason about, and that everything a hand kernel encodes can be recovered as either an algebraic rewrite or a schedule choice. tang’s tensor IR (tang_compile::tensor, about 24k lines of Rust) has two primitives:

  • Views, affine index maps that express broadcast, transpose, slicing, striding, padding and per-head column selection without touching memory.
  • Comprehensions, , where body is a scalar expression graph and is a monoid.

Contributions:

  1. An IR in which fusion, flash attention, logsumexp and Welford variance are instances of two general rewrites (§3).
  2. Reverse-mode differentiation that maps comprehensions to comprehensions, including the online-softmax monoid and a proof procedure that removes the stabilizing max’s gradient. Together with nested contractions, this yields an attention backward with no tensors (§4).
  3. Precision as a schedule, with an emulating interpreter that predicts a format choice’s error before it runs (§5).
  4. Lowerings to six targets from shared emitters, and measurements against hand kernels, PyTorch and the memory-bandwidth limit on three chips (§6, §7).

2 Background: tang

tang is a no_std Rust library that started as a geometry and physics math crate. One Scalar trait covers f32, f64 and dual numbers, so a simulation written once yields exact derivatives by changing a type parameter. tang-expr traces Rust closures into a scalar DAG of nine RISC operations, which it can differentiate symbolically, simplify and compile. The tensor IR uses that scalar DAG as its body language, so every comprehension body can be differentiated, simplified and compared structurally. Higher crates (tang-gpu, tang-llm, tang-train) run programs the IR produces.

3 The IR

3.1 Views

A view maps a point of an iteration space to a source tensor’s coordinates with one affine form per source dimension, . Broadcast is a zero form, transpose a permutation, slice an offset, stride a coefficient. A form with two terms expresses “head reads columns “. Views compose by substitution. They are the only way a comprehension reads its inputs, so every layout decision is a view and every view can be analyzed.

A view may be guarded: an out-of-range read returns the monoid’s identity (zero) rather than raising a shape error. Padded, dilated and transposed (polyphase) convolutions and overlap-add are guarded reads, with no padding operator. The gradient of a guarded read removes a coordinate rather than scattering. For , reads through a guard, so padding contributions never reach .

Grouped-query attention’s is not affine. The IR expresses heads as two dimensions, , and the views do the rest. Dimensions can be fixed or symbolic (Dim::Sym("seq")). Construction, rewriting and differentiation work with symbols, and bind makes them concrete for scheduling and lowering.

3.2 Comprehensions and monoids

A map is a comprehension with no reduce dimensions. Sum and max are monoids. Matmul is . Attention scores, convolution and gather are the same operation with different views. The monoid’s combine is itself a scalar graph, and a comprehension may carry tuple-valued state. Online softmax is the monoid on ; arg-max is the monoid on .

3.3 Rewrites

Fusion is one rule. A map is a pure function of its inputs, so substitute_maps splices its body into every consumer and composes the views. Map→map, map→reduce and transpose→anything are the same case. The rule is cost-aware: a map is not recomputed inside a reduction unless it is cheap. Reductions are never substituted. They are inlined by a schedule decision, which separates algebra from scheduling. A row-major reshape that only merges dimensions is a view, not a copy.

Epilogues ride the reduction. A map that reads a reduction point for point (a bias, an activation, the that finishes softmax) runs on the thread that holds the final accumulator.

Siblings share a pass. Two reductions over the same space and monoid with the same readers become one reduction under the product monoid (merge_siblings). The gate and up projections of a SwiGLU MLP become one tuple-valued matmul, the activation becomes its epilogue, and the normalized input is read once.

Flash attention is a rewrite. stabilize_softmax finds and every over the same canonical , with masks and scales allowed, and replaces them with one reduction under the online-softmax monoid. The value column of becomes a point dimension of the fused comprehension. Nothing is materialized. Logsumexp, Welford variance and top- are the same rewrite with different monoids. 3.4 Schedules

A comprehension says what to compute. Schedule fields say how, and they cannot change the answer:

  • inline: recompute inside the consumer instead of writing a buffer.
  • split: one workgroup per point, with threads striding the reduce space and tree-combining. This is valid for any monoid because combine is associative, including online softmax.
  • tile, tile_2d: blocks of threads, each owning a register tile of points. The emitter tracks every address as an affine form over loop variables. Anything that does not depend on the lane axis is staged in workgroup memory once per reduce chunk. That covers buffer reads (K and V tiles, matmul operand tiles) and inlined sub-computations (the scores).
  • precision: storage and accumulation formats (§5).

schedule_auto encodes three rules distilled from measurement. autotune_kernels times each candidate mapping per materialized node on the device, using the graph’s real inputs, and caches the winner by device and kernel source. With substitution and the online-softmax rewrite, the staging rule produces flash attention’s real structure: rows × lanes per workgroup, cooperative score tiles, and online softmax in registers. Nobody wrote that kernel.

4 Gradients are comprehensions

For , the gradient with respect to through a pick view is found by renaming coordinates. The coordinates that picks become ‘s output dimensions, the rest become reduce dimensions, and every other view is pushed through the same renaming. The body is differentiated by tang-expr’s symbolic diff. A dimension read through any other form (an offset, stride, diagonal or broadcast) stays an output dimension, and the body is gated by through iota, a node that turns coordinates into values. Max gates its term to the arg-max. The backward graph is an ordinary graph, so the same passes optimize it. Rotary embedding and GQA gradients come from the gated scatter with no special handling.

5 Precision is a schedule

f16 is a different way to run the same program, not a different program. Each node has a storage format, applied where a value is written to memory, and an accumulation format, applied to a reduction’s running state after each combine. The graph stays exact math.

The interpreter predicts the error. eval is pure f64. eval_emulated rounds every stored value and every accumulator step to its scheduled format (round to nearest even, subnormals, overflow), with body arithmetic in f64. An ErrorBudget accepts or rejects a schedule before it runs. Emulation matches the GPU’s f16 kernels to 1 ulp when the reduction order is the same. Reduction order matters: AdaIN’s variance over with an f16 accumulator has error under a 256-thread split and with one thread per row, while the sequential emulation predicts . Emulation models the worst case. tune_precision lowers buffers to half precision one move at a time and keeps a move only if the program stays within budget and gets faster.

6 Lowering

Shared emitters produce the point, split and tiled schedules, register tiles, guarded views, epilogue fusion and tuple monoids once. A dialect switch then handles buffer access and surface syntax.

  • WGSL (portable default, through wgpu on Metal, Vulkan and DX12). f16 storage is packed two per u32, because the toolchain rejects enable f16.
  • CUDA. Matmul-shaped tiles run on mma.sync (TF32 m16n8k8, or f16/bf16 m16n8k16 when the operands are half precision) with cp.async staging rings. Block shapes are tuning candidates. cuda_flash_kernel recognizes the online-softmax comprehension and keeps , the row max and the partial sums in mma registers. The score accumulator is reused as the A operand of after permuting keys within each 8-wide step, which is legal because the reduction is associative.
  • Metal (MSL). Native half and bfloat. Contractions with f16 operands run on simdgroup_half8x8 fragments. Reduce dimensions are flattened, so convolution becomes implicit GEMM, with dilated inputs staged once per chunk as a zero-padded, phase-de-interleaved window.
  • OpenCL and GLSL for Adreno GPUs that have neither CUDA nor Metal. On one Adreno driver, WGSL is routed through GLSL because the direct SPIR-V path was miscompiled.
  • C, for batch-1 control loops where a GPU round trip costs more than the arithmetic. A comprehension already is a loop nest, so to_c emits it directly with a vectorizable innermost point dimension, and tang_run() performs no allocation.

Program::tuned times each kernel’s native lowering against the portable one and keeps the faster. Kernel sources are canonicalized, so the same kernel at another layer is the same string and compiles once.

7 Evaluation

Setup. Apple M4 Max (Metal), NVIDIA RTX 3090 (Vulkan and CUDA 12), and a Qualcomm QCS8550 in a Booster K1 humanoid robot (Adreno 740, Cortex-X3). PyTorch 2.10 + cu128 with CUDA events. Unless stated otherwise, tang times are kernel-only. Several runs shared the GPU with other tenants. We mark those, and report minima rather than means, because contention only adds time. Every derived result is checked against an f64 interpreter; agreement is to relative, depending on format.

7.1 Derived vs hand-written kernels

tang shipped hand-written WGSL kernels before the IR existed, which makes a controlled comparison possible: same runtime, same chip, same inputs. Table 1 is one consistent run on the M4 Max, including about 1.8 ms of submit and readback per measurement.

Every hand kernel is matched or beaten. The schedules that win differ across operators and sizes. On the 3090 under Vulkan, the derived transformer block first lost to the hand path. Three chip-agnostic fixes followed: cost-aware substitution, keeping reductions schedulable under their epilogues, and a bank-conflict-free tile layout. After them, the autotuned block won there too (2.5 vs 3.1 ms at seq 128, 6.2 vs 10.4 ms at seq 512).

7.2 Against PyTorch

Table 2 tracks a 9-head GQA transformer block (, , rotary embeddings, SwiGLU) on the RTX 3090 as the backends matured. Through WGSL, which has no access to tensor cores, derived kernels stayed 2.5–4× behind PyTorch. With the CUDA lowering, flash attention on mma.sync and the backward passes of §4, the forward block passes PyTorch (Figure 2).

On the CUDA path, the derived inference attention kernel is 0.032 ms at seq 512, down from 0.178 ms before the mma.sync rewrite. The seq-512 forward block is 1.37× faster than PyTorch with TF32. The training step is at parity or slightly ahead. We do not claim a general win over cuBLAS: a lone fp32 matmul at through WGSL reaches 7.6 TFLOPS against cuBLAS’s roughly 19. The gain comes from fusion across operator boundaries that a library of separately tuned operators cannot do.

7.3 A whole model as one graph

Qwen3-8B with MLX 4-bit weights runs as one IR graph per decode step, lowered by to_cuda_decode into 506 kernels and captured as a CUDA graph. Nibble unpacking and the dequantization are a map the GEMV reads. That map is inlined and evaluated in registers as weights stream in. Figure 3 shows where the remaining time goes. The KV-cache update is a select over a runtime position, which the lowering turns into loop bounds, so a step touches only live cache rows.

The same graph builder lowers to Metal. There, prefill and decode programs share one copy of the weights through buffer binding, prefill can start from a nonzero offset (300 + 200 tokens is bit-identical to 500), and the next token is chosen on the device by an arg-max monoid, so decoding reads back 8 bytes per token.

7.4 Edge hardware

On the K1′s Adreno 740, a derived matmul reaches 322 GFLOP/s at , which is 21% of the measured fp32 peak, and a full transformer forward pass plus all seven weight gradients runs in 19.1 ms. Rules tuned for desktop GPUs under-fill the Adreno’s six compute units. The best schedule there (an register tile with 8-deep chunks) is 1.45× better than schedule_auto’s choice, which again argues for search over remembered rules. Through to_c on one Cortex-X3 core, a batch-1 GRU policy step (6 contractions plus gate math, fused into one kernel) takes 22.5 µs median and a locomotion MLP takes 6.7 µs, both within of f64.

8 Verification

Speed claims depend on every kernel being correct. tang-verify runs each GPU kernel against its CPU oracle on the same input, starting from the device’s own state. Before each kernel runs, it fills the kernel’s outputs with NaN, so unwritten memory cannot pass as a plausible 0. When a check fails, it identifies the responsible kernel by provenance: whose correct values ended up in this buffer. It was built after four structural bugs in a hand-written engine slipped past every unit test. One was a byte offset added where a float offset was meant, which wrote k’s values over q. Derived kernels are less exposed to that class of bug, because addresses come from affine forms rather than pointer arithmetic. The IR path also has a structural oracle: the f64 interpreter runs the identical graph.

9 Related work

Halide 2 separated algorithm from schedule. TVM 3 and Ansor 4 added learned and searched schedules. Tensor Comprehensions 5 used polyhedral comprehensions. tang inherits the separation and the search. What it adds is monoid-valued comprehensions whose combine is an analyzable scalar graph, which turns online softmax into a rewrite rather than a template, and differentiation inside the IR, so the backward pass is also derived and scheduled. Triton 6 makes hand kernels easier to write; tang tries to make them unnecessary. FlashAttention 1,7 and its backward are hand derivations of what §3 and §4 produce mechanically. Online softmax itself is due to Milakov and Gimelshein 8. XLA and TorchInductor fuse elementwise chains around library calls. tang has no library calls to fuse around.

10 Limitations

  • Measurement conditions. Several key runs (the Qwen3 decode and the flash-backward kernels) were taken on shared GPUs. We report minima, which bound the code’s speed from below, but they are not idle-machine numbers.
  • No real cost model. schedule_auto is three measured rules, and the autotuner is the honest answer until a cost model exists.
  • Non-affine indexing. Paged KV caches and MoE routing need a gather view that reads an index tensor, and that view blocks some fusion.
  • Scatter gradients reduce over the whole source coordinate they gate on, so a stride-2 read pays 2× in the backward pass.
  • Body arithmetic is f32 on every path. Storage and accumulators honor f16.
  • Distributed placement exists as a rewrite (sharded reductions joined by an all-reduce expressed as a comprehension over the monoid’s combine), but transport goes through the host.

11 Conclusion

Two primitives, affine views and monoid comprehensions, are enough to express a transformer, its gradient and its quantized inference. Fusion, flash attention and the flash backward are rewrites and schedule choices over those primitives, not kernels. Because the schedule is searched per chip, the same source beats hand kernels on Metal, Vulkan and an embedded Adreno, and passes PyTorch on a transformer block on CUDA. A hand-written kernel does not disappear in this design. It becomes an intrinsic: a known-fast implementation of one comprehension shape on one chip, which the compiler uses when it wins and replaces when it doesn’t.
